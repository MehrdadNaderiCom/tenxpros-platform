## 1. Short context

TenXPros is a **selective AI adoption certification for experienced professionals** — not a tools course and not a university program. Each participant brings **one real professional problem** from their own field, and TenXPros provides the **AI method** to work it through responsibly. The output is a **Living AI Solution Dossier**: a structured, reviewed artifact covering the problem, boundaries, workflow, evaluation, value, governance, and a 90-day roadmap. **Certification is earned through reviewed evidence, not attendance** — a dossier is reviewed against eight explicit, public criteria, and the credential is verifiable. **Payment happens only after acceptance**: applicants apply first with a real problem, and no payment is requested unless they are accepted. The **Founding Charter is currently open** for the first 10 accepted members at $997.

The core thread throughout: **"You bring the expertise. We bring the AI method."**

## 2. Reviewer instruction

Please review the **live site**, walking the full journey from **Home → Program/Method → Dossier → Certification → Pricing → Apply → About**, and open the **sample dossier** (PDF and HTML preview).

## 4. Core review questions (20)

**Positioning clarity**
1. Within ~5 seconds on the Home page, is it clear *what* TenXPros is and *who* it is for?
2. Is the distinction "lead AI adoption / don't just use AI" meaningful and credible, or does it read as a slogan?
3. After Home + Program, do you clearly understand the TenX Method (Frame · Design · Prove · Foresee) and the 12-week / 4-phase / 11-module + capstone structure?

**Trust and credibility**
4. Does the site earn trust *before* asking for anything (sample dossier, public criteria, verification, "pay after acceptance")?
5. Is the founder/architect credibility presented as a *trust layer* (not the headline), and does it strengthen or distract?
6. Is anything missing that a serious professional would need to trust the credential (e.g., proof, examples, verification)?

**Pricing and value**
7. Does the $997 Founding Charter feel like a *serious, premium* opportunity rather than a cheap course?
8. Is the value stack ("what you're paying for is not a video course") convincing for the price?
9. Is the charter ladder (Founding → Standard) credible and non-gimmicky (no fake scarcity/countdowns)?

**Dossier and certification logic**
10. After seeing the sample dossier, do you understand what participants actually produce?
11. Are the eight review criteria and three outcomes (Certified / Strong Draft / Completed) clear and fair?
12. Does "earned through reviewed evidence, not attendance" come across as real, or as marketing?

**UI/UX and visual quality**
13. Does the site feel like one premium product across all pages (dark "Instrument" style, consistent CTAs)?
14. Is the experience excellent on mobile (readability, navigation, no clutter)?

**Apply flow and conversion**
15. Is "apply first, pay only after acceptance" unmistakable before the form?
16. Does the application form feel appropriately serious but not intimidating, and is it clear no payment details are needed to apply?

**Claims safety and credibility risk**
17. Did you see any claim that felt exaggerated, unsupported, or risky (e.g., guaranteed outcomes, accreditation, "better than universities")?
18. Does the site stay honest about what it is *not* (not a degree, not accreditation, not a guarantee of jobs/income)?

**International professional audience fit**
19. Would this resonate with experienced professionals *outside* one country/industry (consultants, managers, researchers, regulated fields)?
20. Is the English clear, professional, and free of culture-specific or hype-y phrasing that could alienate an international audience?

## 6. Red-flag checklist

Please flag anything that applies, with the page/URL and a short note:

- [ ] A claim that sounds exaggerated or unsupported
- [ ] A page that feels confusing or hard to follow
- [ ] A pricing concern (too high / too low / unclear / feels risky)
- [ ] A trust gap (something you'd need to believe before applying)
- [ ] A visual inconsistency (a page that feels "off" vs the rest)
- [ ] A mobile issue (overflow, clipped buttons, unreadable text, broken menu)
- [ ] An unclear or weak CTA
- [ ] An unsupported credibility claim (numbers, titles, affiliations)
- [ ] Any reason a serious professional would hesitate to apply
